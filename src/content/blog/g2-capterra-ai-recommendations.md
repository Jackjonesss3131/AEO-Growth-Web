**The short answer:** review profiles get you *considered*, not cited. AI engines appear to check that you exist as a real product in a real category before naming you — and then cite something else entirely. That should change what you expect from the spend, not whether you spend it.

This comes up in almost every audit I run for B2B SaaS. A team is paying real money for review platforms and can't tell whether it's doing anything for AI visibility.

## What I see in audits

Two patterns show up consistently.

**Companies absent from G2 and Capterra rarely get named** in answers about their category, even when their own content is good.

**Companies present on those platforms get named — but the cited link is usually somewhere else.** A Reddit thread, a comparison article, a competitor's buyer's guide.

Both being true at once is what confuses people. Your profile is doing work; it's just not the work you can see in a citation.

Think of it as a gate rather than a source. Before an engine puts you in an answer, something in its process verifies you're a real product with a category, structured attributes and third-party signal. Review profiles are where that's easiest to check. Passing the check gets you into the room. It doesn't get you the link.

## Why the published numbers disagree

If you go looking, you'll find studies saying review platforms appear in about a third of Google's AI answers, and others finding zero G2 or Capterra citations across hundreds of ChatGPT recommendations.

Both are probably right. They measured different engines. Google's AI results surface long source lists; ChatGPT cites far more sparingly. Perplexity sits between them.

The practical takeaway isn't a number. It's that you should check your own category rather than trusting anyone's average, including mine.

## What actually moves the needle

**Category placement beats review volume.** Being in the right category, positioned well, matters more than collecting another fifty reviews. G2's own analysis found review count explains very little of the variance in citations — they published that themselves, which is worth something.

**Your description on the profile matters more than your rating.** This is the failure I find most often. A company repositions, updates its homepage in a day, and never touches its G2 category. The profile keeps describing the old product, in the old category, to the old buyer — and it keeps passing the eligibility check with that description. The model verifies you exist as the thing you used to be.

No amount of new reviews fixes that. It's an entity problem.

**Presence is close to a prerequisite.** Which is why "cancel the contract" is the wrong conclusion from any of this.

## Where to put the budget instead

If you're deciding between another review-generation push and something else, this is the order I'd suggest.

1. **Fix the descriptions you already have.** Every profile, listing and directory entry, checked against what you sell today. Cheapest and highest impact if you've repositioned.

2. **Get the category right.** On G2, Capterra and anywhere else you're listed. Wrong category is worse than no category — it actively teaches the model something false.

3. **Then think about volume.** Reviews help at the margin, once the first two are right.

## What gets cited instead

Community discussion and third-party comparison lists consistently outweigh review directories as actual citation sources. In most B2B SaaS categories, the buyer's guides the models read were written by vendors — often your competitors.

That's separate work from review platforms, and usually the higher-leverage one. I go through how to find which sources are shaping your specific answers in [What Sources Does AI Cite](/blog/what-sources-does-ai-cite/).

## One distinction worth holding onto

Being named in an AI answer and being the link that gets clicked are very different outcomes. Most companies named in a recommendation don't get the citation pointing at their own site — it points at whatever page the engine used as its source.

So "we appear in AI answers" and "AI sends us traffic" are separate claims. If your goal is the second, the work is on the pages that get cited, not on your review profile.

## Quick answers

**Do G2 reviews help AI visibility?**
Presence functions as an eligibility signal. Tools that AI recommends almost always have review profiles, but those profiles are rarely the cited source.

**How many reviews do I need?**
Fewer than you'd think. Category placement and an accurate description matter considerably more than volume.

**Should we keep paying for review platforms?**
Usually yes — for search visibility, buyer trust and procurement. Just budget it as an eligibility and trust investment, not as an AI citation strategy.

**What's the most common mistake?**
A profile that still describes the company you were two years ago. It passes every check with the wrong description.
