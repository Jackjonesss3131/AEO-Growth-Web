**The short answer:** the evidence genuinely conflicts, and anyone giving you a confident yes or no hasn't read it. The most defensible reading is that review platforms work as an *eligibility gate* rather than a *citation source* — AI engines check whether you exist and how you're described on G2 and Capterra before naming you, but often cite something else. For a B2B SaaS spending real money on review-site programs, that distinction changes what you should expect from the spend.

This matters more since February 2026, when [G2 acquired Capterra, Software Advice and GetApp from Gartner](https://company.g2.com/news/g2-acquires-capterra-software-advice-getapp), putting most of the review-platform category under one company. In its own announcement, G2 frames the combined dataset as source-of-truth infrastructure for the LLMs and agents that will inform go-to-market strategy — which tells you how the seller sees it.

Here's what the research actually shows.

## The studies that say yes

**Review platforms appear in about a third of AI Overviews.** [SE Ranking analyzed 30,000 commercial keywords](https://seranking.com/blog/review-platforms-in-ai-overviews/) and tracked 23 review platforms: review platforms appeared in 34.5% of AI Overview responses and received 8.5% of all links, with Gartner Peer Insights, G2 and Capterra leading.

**Category position correlates with citation frequency.** [Presenc AI's monitoring](https://presenc.ai/research/does-g2-capterra-reviews-improve-ai-visibility-2026) across procurement-intent queries found that brands in the top 20 on G2 or Capterra for their primary category are cited roughly 3.1 times more often in "best [category]" answers than brands absent from those platforms.

**Near-universal presence among recommended tools.** [A 2026 analysis of software category queries](https://www.madx.digital/learn/review-sites-ai-recommendations) found every tool ChatGPT named had Capterra reviews and 99% had G2 reviews.

**Buyers trust the citation when it appears.** The same analysis reports 45% of buyers saying a review-site citation is the most confidence-inspiring signal in an AI response.

## The studies that say no

**Zero citations across 233 recommendations.** A study by DerivateX — 40 B2B SaaS categories, one buyer-intent prompt each, ten runs per prompt in fresh chats, ChatGPT with web search, every cited URL logged — produced 233 recommendations across 219 distinct tools. [As reported in a breakdown of the data](https://lymlyt.pro/blog/g2-capterra-ai-visibility/), review aggregators accounted for 0.9% of all citations, and G2 and Capterra each received **zero**.

**Directories at 0.7% of citations, Reddit at 13%.** [A separate measurement of 40 buying questions](https://glotier.com/guides/what-sources-ai-cites), logging 292 citations across 196 domains, found Reddit appeared in 38 of the 40 answers. G2 appeared in 2. Capterra in none. The five classic software review directories together accounted for 0.7% of citations — Reddit alone was roughly nineteen times their combined share.

**G2's own data shows a weak relationship.** This is the one that should carry the most weight, because it cuts against the publisher's interest. [As documented in one review of the evidence](https://strivelabs.ai/blog/g2-capterra-ai-answers/), G2's own analysis found a 10% increase in reviews associated with 2% more citations — a relationship explaining under 1% of the variance. Volume helps slightly. It isn't the lever.

**Placement is weak even when links appear.** When review platform links do show up, only about 36% land in the top three source positions, with the median around fifth, and roughly 93% appear in the sources block rather than inline in the answer text.

> **"How can both sets of findings be true?"**
>
> Mostly because they measure different things.
>
> The "yes" studies lean on Google AI Overviews and Perplexity, which surface source lists heavily. The "no" studies measure ChatGPT with web search, which cites differently. Perplexity appears to lean on G2 more than ChatGPT does.
>
> They also measure different questions. Broad category queries pull comparison and review pages. Constrained questions — a specific stack, a specific team size — pull different sources entirely.
>
> And one measures presence while the other measures citation. Every tool ChatGPT recommended had review profiles. Almost none of those profiles were the cited link. Both statements are true at once, and they point to the resolution.

## The resolution: gate, not source

The interpretation that fits all of the data is that review platforms function as an **eligibility check** rather than a citation source.

Before an engine names your software, something in its process verifies that you exist as a real product in a real category, with structured attributes and third-party signal. G2 and Capterra profiles are where that verification is easiest — heavily indexed, structured, current. When the check passes, the engine may cite something else entirely: a Reddit thread, a comparison article, a vendor's guide.

Two observations make this concrete.

**Absence from these platforms looks close to absence from the answer**, even in studies where the platforms themselves are barely cited.

**Traffic collapse hasn't changed citation share.** Traffic to the major review platforms fell between 76% and 92% over two years while their citation share held. Buyers are getting the information through AI answers instead of visiting.

## The stat that should reframe your spend

From the same ChatGPT study: the engine attached a citation to 92.3% of the tools it named — but only **11.6% of those citations pointed at the recommended tool's own website**.

Read that twice if you're planning content budget.

Being named is nearly automatic once you're eligible. Being *cited* — having the link that captures the click — is a different and much rarer outcome. A buyer can hear your name in an answer and click straight through to a Reddit thread or a competitor's comparison page.

This is the same distinction I wrote about in [How to Get AI Recommendations](/blog/how-to-get-ai-recommendations/): mention, citation and recommendation are three different results, and a single "visibility" number collapses them into one.

> **"So should we cancel our G2 contract?"**
>
> No — and I want to be careful here, because the contrarian read is easy to over-apply.
>
> Review platforms still rank in ordinary search, still carry buyer intent, and a category leader badge still closes deals with procurement. G2's 2025 buyer research put GenAI chatbots as the top influence on B2B shortlists at 17.1%, with software review sites right behind at 15.1%. They work as a pair: AI assembles the shortlist, review sites are where the buyer checks it.
>
> What the data argues against is treating review-site spend as an *AI citation* strategy. Budget it as an eligibility requirement and a human trust signal — both real — and stop expecting it to be the thing that makes ChatGPT cite you.

## What this means for B2B SaaS budget

Five practical conclusions.

**1. Get on the platforms. Don't expect them to be the citation.**
Presence appears to be close to a prerequisite. Being the cited source is a different game, played mostly on other surfaces.

**2. Category placement matters more than review volume.**
Top-20 position in your category correlates with roughly 3x more citations, while review volume explains under 1% of the variance by G2's own numbers. Getting categorized correctly beats collecting another fifty reviews — and category accuracy is exactly what breaks after a repositioning.

**3. Check your own category before committing budget.**
Which platform gets cited varies by category and engine. Run your own buyer questions and look at the sources before signing an annual contract. That's a free hour of work against a five-figure decision.

**4. Take Reddit seriously.**
Across the citation studies, Reddit repeatedly outweighs every review directory combined. Not as a place to seed threads — models and moderators both handle that badly — but as a place where your category is genuinely discussed and where your absence is conspicuous.

**5. Your own site still has to do the work.**
If only 11.6% of citations point at the recommended vendor's own site, the pages that do earn that link are the specific ones: integration pages, honest comparisons, pricing with real context, customer results with numbers. Generic feature pages don't get cited by anyone.

## Where review profiles quietly break

The most common failure I see in audits isn't absence. It's a profile that's still accurate about the company you were two years ago.

A repositioning propagates to your homepage in a day and to your G2 category never. The profile keeps describing the old product, in the old category, to the old buyer — and it keeps passing the eligibility check with that description. The model verifies you exist as the thing you used to be.

That's not a review-volume problem and no amount of new reviews fixes it. It's an entity problem, and it needs someone to go through every profile, listing and directory entry and correct them against what you actually sell today. ## Quick answers

**Do G2 reviews help with AI visibility?**
Evidence suggests presence on G2 functions as an eligibility signal rather than a citation source. Tools recommended by ChatGPT almost universally have review profiles, but those profiles are rarely the cited link. Google AI Overviews and Perplexity cite review platforms more than ChatGPT does.

**How many G2 reviews do I need for AI to recommend me?**
Volume appears to be a weak lever. G2's own analysis found a 10% increase in reviews associated with 2% more citations, explaining under 1% of the variance. Category placement correlates far more strongly than review count.

**Does Capterra matter for AI recommendations?**
It's part of the same eligibility layer, and now the same company as G2 following the February 2026 acquisition. In ChatGPT citation studies it appears very rarely as a cited source.

**What gets cited instead of review sites?**
Reddit consistently outweighs the review directories by a wide margin in citation studies, alongside comparison articles, vendor-published buyer's guides and community sources.

**Should B2B SaaS still pay for review platforms?**
Usually yes — for search visibility, buyer trust and procurement. Just budget it as an eligibility and trust investment rather than an AI citation strategy, and verify which platforms get cited in your specific category first.
