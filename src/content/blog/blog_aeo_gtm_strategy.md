# How to Implement AEO in Your GTM

**The short answer:** don't try to cover the whole funnel. Start at BOFU. Take your product, ask who would use it and in what specific case, and turn that into the questions your buyer asks the model right before deciding. Those questions are your plan. Everything else is noise until you win those.

## Why BOFU, not awareness

Implementing AEO across your entire GTM at once is unpredictable. Too many questions, too many engines, too much you don't control. Start with awareness — the broad category questions — and you're fighting the incumbent on the ground where it wins, and you'll wait months to see if anything moved.

BOFU is the opposite. Fewer questions, more specific, and the highest intent you'll ever touch. It's where the budget is. And it's where the model hands over the shortlist: when your buyer asks what to use for their case, not what your category is.

So the approach is simple: win your niche's decision questions first. Those you can actually win, and they're the ones that move pipeline.

## Step 1: start from your product, not keywords

Don't open a keyword tool. Open your product and ask two questions:

**Who would use it?** Not "B2B companies." The real buyer: their role, team size, stage, stack, budget.

**In what case would they reach for it?** The specific moment your product is the answer. The problem that makes them search today, not in the abstract.

Each "who + case" combination is a segment of your ICP. And each segment asks the model a different question. That's where you start.

## Step 2: turn that into your buyer's questions

Take each "who + case" and write it the way your buyer would ask ChatGPT or Perplexity, with the context attached.

Not: "best attribution tool?"
Yes: "what attribution tool should I use for a 20-person B2B SaaS on HubSpot with a tight budget?"

The second is the real BOFU question. It carries context, it carries constraints, and the answer is a short shortlist where you either are or aren't. Make a list of those questions — there are usually fewer than you'd think, somewhere between five and fifteen cover most of your pipeline.

Those questions are your backlog. Not the one a volume tool suggests: the one that comes out of your own product and your own buyer.

## Step 3: build the pages that decide the answer

The model answers those questions with what it read on pages that, mostly, aren't yours. Your job is to be one of those sources, in the right category.

The BOFU pages the model uses to decide:

- **Comparisons** — you vs the competitor that shows up in the shortlist
- **Integrations** — "works with [your ICP's stack]"
- **Segment cases** — one page per "who + case," not one generic page for everyone
- **Pricing and proof** — what your buyer validates right before they call

One page per question, with the direct answer up top. Your blog explains things; these pages get you bought. More on the difference between appearing and being recommended in [How to Get AI Recommendations (Not Just Citations)](https://www.aeogrowth.co/blog/how-to-get-ai-recommendations/).

## Step 4: check they can read you, and measure monthly

Two things before you call this done.

First, that AI can read those pages. Most crawlers don't run JavaScript — if your pages don't read without JS, they don't exist to the model. Check it in two minutes with the [crawler check](https://www.aeogrowth.co/ai-crawler-check/).

Second, that you're described right when you appear. Making the shortlist isn't enough: if the model describes you in the category you already moved away from, you appear but you lose. That's why measurement is per engine and per question, every month — ChatGPT, Perplexity, Gemini and Google answer differently and keep changing. What worked in March stops working.

## Quick answers

**Where do I start an AEO strategy for GTM?** At BOFU. Take your product, define who uses it and in what case, and build your buyer's decision questions. Those are your plan.

**How many questions do I need?** Few. Between five and fifteen BOFU questions, with your ICP's context, cover most of the pipeline. You don't need a hundred.

**Blog or service pages?** Both, but don't confuse them. The blog explains; the comparison, integration, case and pricing pages are what the model uses to recommend you.

**How do I know it's working?** Run your BOFU questions across every engine, every month. See whether you're in the shortlist and how you're described. If you're not there, that page is the next one you build.
