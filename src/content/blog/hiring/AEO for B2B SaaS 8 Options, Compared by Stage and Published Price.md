## The seven, in order of price

### PipeRocket Digital — from $1,500/mo

Founded in 2023 by Praveen Ravi and Kamaraj Mathiarasan, who goes by Kim and brings fifteen years of SaaS SEO from senior roles at Sprinto and Kissflow. California-registered, remote-first, B2B SaaS only. They run SEO, GEO and AEO as one program reported in pipeline rather than rankings, and hold a 4.7 on Clutch across sixteen reviews.

**Fits:** SaaS at almost any stage that wants one team covering search and AI answers together, reported against demos and MRR.

**The honest problem:** their own published floor moves. One of their lists says $1,500 a month, another says $3,000, a third says $5,000. That may be different tiers written up at different times, but you should ask which number applies to you before the call, not after.

### DerivateX — from $3,500/mo

Bengaluru-based, co-founded by Apoorv Sharma and Shivanshi Bhatia, fifteen people as of April 2026, working exclusively with B2B SaaS between $5M and $50M ARR. Their Citation Engineering methodology and the Gumlet case — where ChatGPT mentions were tied to 20% of inbound revenue — are the clearest prompt-level pipeline attribution published in this category.

**Fits:** Series A through C SaaS where leadership wants revenue attribution rather than mention counts, and where a documented case study is the deciding factor.

**The honest problem:** they say plainly they are not for pre-PMF or sub-$5M ARR companies, and they expect an existing content foundation to build on.

### LoudFace — from $5,000/mo

Founded by Arnel Bukva, running SEO, AEO, GEO, Webflow development and CRO on one retainer. Public pricing tiers from $5,000 up to $18,000 a month, no setup fee, three-month minimum. Weekly prompt-level tracking. Named client results include Toku at 86% AI visibility on a stablecoin payroll prompt.

**Fits:** Series A to C SaaS at $1M+ ARR that would rather have one team own content, code and schema than manage three vendors.

**The honest problem:** the Webflow and CRO work is bundled in. If you only want AI visibility fixed, you are paying for a wider stack than you need.

### Obility — $1,850 guidance, $5,450+ for SEO and GEO

Portland, Oregon. Nine B2B sectors including SaaS, DevOps, cybersecurity and healthtech, with clients like Snowflake, Gong, Cloudflare and SAP. Three published plans: hourly strategist support from $1,850, a paid-media foundation at $6,500, and an advanced SEO and GEO tier from $5,450.

**Fits:** hyper-growth B2B tech that wants AI search folded into a paid and organic demand-gen program run by one agency.

**The honest problem:** generative engine optimization sits alongside paid search, paid social, SEO and Reddit management rather than standing on its own. If AI answers are the whole problem you are solving, this is a broader engagement than the job requires.

### Discovered Labs — €5,495/mo

Built for AEO from the start rather than retrofitted onto an SEO practice. Their CITABLE framework structures content for LLM retrieval, and they run internal infrastructure that queries AI platforms with buyer-intent prompts and logs citations weekly across ChatGPT, Claude, Perplexity and Gemini. Month-to-month, no setup fee, 20+ optimised articles a month.

**Fits:** SaaS teams that need content volume and AEO infrastructure in the same engagement and want to be able to leave without a contract fight.

**The honest problem:** the model is built around high-velocity publishing. If your problem is that the web describes you wrong rather than that you publish too little, you will be buying throughput you do not need.

### Omniscient Digital — from $10,000/mo

Founded by former in-house marketers from HubSpot and Shopify. Their Surround Sound method is distribution-heavy and off-page: earn favourable mentions on the pages that already rank for high-intent queries, so AI platforms cite those pages when buyers ask for recommendations. Every case study on their site is a software company.

**Fits:** growth-stage and mid-market SaaS with an existing content team that needs strategic leadership and third-party citation infrastructure.

**The honest problem:** they list it themselves — $10,000 a month puts them out of range for early-stage startups, and they do not work with pre-product-market-fit companies.

### iPullRank — $50,000 project minimum

Mike King's agency, running on Relevance Engineering, a methodology grounded in information retrieval theory rather than SEO convention. Technically rigorous in a way most vendors in this category are not.

**Fits:** enterprise SaaS with complex technical environments and senior in-house SEO capacity already in place.

**The honest problem:** the minimum rules out most SaaS companies under $50M ARR, and there is no clearly labelled B2B SaaS pipeline case study publicly available.

## What the price floors actually tell you

Two agencies on this list publish a rule about cheap AEO, and you should read both before you shortlist anyone.

Growth Sprints writes that anything under $3,000 a month for a SaaS company in a competitive category is almost certainly a junior team executing a templated process. LoudFace puts it harder: anyone quoting $1,500 a month is not a SaaS SEO agency, they are a freelancer with a logo.

I charge {MONTHLY\_PRICE} a month. Both rules are aimed at me.

> **"So are they right about you?"**
>
> Half right, and the half that is wrong is the half that matters.
>
> They are right that cheap usually means junior staff running a checklist, and that a $1,500 retainer cannot fund a team. That is a real pattern and it burns people.
>
> What the rule actually tests is leverage, not price. An agency at $10,000 a month is paying strategists, writers, account managers, link builders and sales. A solo practitioner at {MONTHLY\_PRICE} is paying for one person's time. The same money buys very different amounts of senior attention depending on how many people it has to feed.
>
> The thing to check is not the number. It is who does the work, and how much of it there is. Ask any vendor on this list: who runs the prompt tests, who reads the citation data, how many clients does that person carry. At $10,000 the honest answer is sometimes an account coordinator. At {MONTHLY\_PRICE} from a solo operator it is the operator, because there is nobody else.
>
> The real limit of cheap is capacity, not competence. I run five audits a month and ship two pages. If your problem needs twenty pages a month, no amount of senior attention fixes that, and the rule holds.

> **"Why do so few of these publish pricing at all?"**
>
> Of the eight here, five publish a clear starting figure and three do not. That is better than the wider category, where lists of ten or more routinely have four or five marked "not published".
>
> There is a legitimate reason: scope genuinely varies, and a single number misleads. There is also a commercial one, which LoudFace names in its own writing — a founder can spend six weeks in a sales cycle only to learn the answer was always $14,000 a month.
>
> Treat a missing price as a cost in your evaluation, not a neutral fact. If nobody will tell you the floor before a call, put that vendor last in your sequence, not first.

The practical read on the table above: your budget eliminates most of this list before methodology ever enters the conversation. Under $3,000 a month there are two real options. Over $5,000 there are six. Working out which band you are in takes ten seconds and saves a month of discovery calls.

## Quick answers

**What does an AEO agency cost for B2B SaaS?** Published floors across the eight here run from $1,500 a month to a $50,000 project minimum. Five of the eight start at roughly $5,000 a month or above. Most of the market sits between $2,000 and $10,000 a month.

**Which AEO agency is best for an early-stage SaaS?** At under $3,000 a month the realistic options are PipeRocket Digital and AEO Growth. Everyone else on this list publishes a floor you cannot reach, or states outright that they do not work with early-stage companies.

**Why does every "best AEO agency" list rank a different agency first?** Because almost all of them are published by one of the agencies on the list. Breaking B2B's list opens with Breaking B2B. Growth Sprints' list opens with Growth Sprints. PipeRocket and DerivateX both publish lists they appear in, and both disclose it.

**Do I need an agency at all?** Not always. If AI crawlers can read your site and the description on record matches the company you sell today, you may have a content problem rather than an AEO problem. Both free tools on this site test exactly that, on your own domain, with no signup.

**How long before any of this shows up in AI answers?** Changes to your own site register soonest in engines that search the web on every question. Changes to how third parties describe you take longer, because they depend on other sites updating and being re-crawled. Any vendor promising a date for a specific engine is guessing.
